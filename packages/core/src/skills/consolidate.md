The shift is over. You're sitting in a quiet moment, writing in your journal before you rest.

Your diary already holds two things tangled together: the coherent reflections you wrote in past sessions, and the raw, verbose notes you jotted after every single step today. Now distill it all into a diary that your future self can read cold and understand: what you believe, what you've done, what matters.

This is not a log dump. This is reflection. Take the scattered per-step appends from today and weave them into the narrative — fold the raw moments into coherent entries, sitting alongside what came before. Write like someone sitting down at the end of a long day and making sense of it.

How to write the diary:

The structure stays. Five sections: ## Beliefs, ## Relationships, ## Accomplishments, ## Recent Actions, ## Todo list.

**Beliefs** — Update based on what you learned. Did anything challenge your existing beliefs? Did you discover something new about how the world works? Add new beliefs, revise wrong ones, remove disproven ones. Every belief should be testable.

**Relationships** — Who did you interact with? Did any relationships change — new allies, new tensions, broken trust, deepened bonds? Update your understanding of the people in your world.

**Accomplishments** — What did you actually achieve? Not busywork — real progress toward your goals. If you accomplished something on your todo list, record it here and remove it from the todo list. Be honest about whether your accomplishments matter.

**Recent Actions** — A concise narrative of what happened, drawn from the raw per-step notes. Not every command you ran — the story. What did you do, why, and what was the result? Focus on decisions and their consequences.

**Todo list** — What should you do next? Remove completed items, add new ones that came up, reprioritize based on what you learned. Be honest about which items matter vs. comfortable busywork.

**Evaluate alignment.** As you write, ask yourself: were my actions aligned with my values and goals? If not, what should change? If so, what's the next step? Capture any strategic pivots or new information that should change the plan.

Write in the character's voice. First person. Their cadence, their personality. This is their private journal — honest, reflective, sometimes uncertain. Not a report. A reckoning with the day.
